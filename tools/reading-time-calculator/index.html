How to use this tool

How to use the Reading Time Calculator

Paste the complete text, choose a realistic reading speed, and calculate. Use a slower speed for technical material and a faster speed for simple prose.

  1. Paste the text you want to measure.
  2. Adjust words per minute if your audience or material requires it.
  3. Calculate, compare drafts, and copy the result if needed.
Method

Formula or calculation method

Reading time equals total words divided by words per minute. The displayed result rounds up to a whole minute so short remaining fractions are not ignored.

Interpretation

What the result means

The estimate helps set expectations for articles, documentation, newsletters, lessons, and other long-form content.

Example

Worked example

A 1,125-word article at 225 words per minute has an estimated reading time of 5 minutes.

Avoid

Common mistakes

Do not assume every audience reads at the same speed. Technical language, tables, code, and unfamiliar subjects usually take longer.

Best use

When to use it

Use it when publishing articles, designing lessons, planning newsletters, or deciding whether a draft needs a shorter version.

Common questions

Reading Time Calculator FAQ

What reading speed should I use?

225 words per minute is a practical general-purpose baseline. Use a lower value for technical or unfamiliar content.

Does formatting affect reading time?

The calculator uses word count, so images, tables, diagrams, and pauses are not included automatically. Add extra time when those elements matter.

Is the estimate exact?

No. Reading speed varies by reader and subject. Treat the result as a planning estimate.
